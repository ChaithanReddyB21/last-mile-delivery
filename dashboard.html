    What the result guarantees

    Model, correctness & complexity

    One vehicle leaves the warehouse with its selected packages and returns after the final stop. A customer deadline constrains service completion, including all preceding travel and service. Roads are undirected with positive travel times. Passing through a customer node does not deliver their package.

    Kruskal builds a minimum spanning forest using distance. It describes the infrastructure; delivery routes use time-weighted shortest paths, not the MST. Dijkstra uses adjacency lists and a binary min-heap. Floyd–Warshall independently computes all-pairs travel times.

    0/1 knapsack maximizes profit subject to integer package weight in O(nW) time and memory. Its profit is an upper bound after relaxing route deadlines. For up to 12 customers, a subset DP keeps the earliest feasible service completion for each (subset, last stop). Earlier completion dominates later completion for identical states, so selecting the highest-profit feasible subset is exact. Time O(2ⁿn²), memory O(2ⁿn).

    Above 12 customers, knapsack candidates enter a feasible insertion heuristic, compared with ratio-based greedy insertion. Every insertion rechecks the full schedule and capacity. The better feasible plan wins; global route optimality is not guaranteed. No live traffic or real geographic mapping is modeled.
